The Artifact Grading System of Aulon

The standardized classification of weapons, relics, tools, magical objects, divine instruments and ancient artifacts—from worthless matter to the unpossessable Hymns.

THE TENFOLD RELIC SCALE

From rarity to divinity

Across Aulon, all weapons, relics, tools, magical objects, divine instruments and ancient artifacts are classified according to the Artifact Grading System.

Guilds, kingdoms, scholars, collectors, artificers and adventurers use the scale to determine not only an item’s rarity, but also its magical potency, historical significance, metaphysical origin and danger to the wielder.

The system begins at ∅ Grade, for objects with no significant value, and ascends through increasingly rare grades until reaching Grade X, a classification reserved for phenomena so impossible that they cannot truly be possessed as items at all.

At lower levels, item grades describe rarity and usefulness.

At higher levels, they describe proximity to divinity.

∅
Rubbish or insignificant items

Null Grade

Commonly found anywhereNo formal value

Null Grade items are objects with no meaningful magical, historical or material value. They are mundane, disposable, damaged or otherwise insignificant.

Such items may be found in homes, markets, ruins, battlefields, trash heaps or abandoned settlements. They are not useless in the practical sense, but possess no value within the formal grading system.

  • Broken tools
  • Rusted knives
  • Worn clothing
  • Cracked pottery
  • Damaged trinkets
  • Poorly made weapons
  • Ordinary household objects

Most civilians interact almost exclusively with Null Grade items throughout their lives.

To adventurers, Null Grade equipment is often a sign of poverty, desperation or inexperience.

1
Approximately 1 in 5

Common Grade

Grade 1 items are slightly superior to ordinary objects and may possess small improvements in durability, craftsmanship, utility or minor magical enhancement.

They are not especially valuable, but useful enough to be recognized by guilds and merchants.

  • A well-balanced sword
  • A reinforced shield
  • A minor charm
  • A fuel-efficient lantern
  • Basic enchanted clothing
  • Simple adventuring gear

Common Grade items are frequently used by beginning adventurers, militia, caravan guards and lower-ranking mercenaries. They may determine whether a novice survives their first journey.

2
Approximately 1 in 50

Uncommon Grade

Grade 2 items possess more noticeable value. They may contain minor enchantments, higher-quality materials or specialized functions, providing a clear advantage over standard equipment.

  • Lightly enchanted weapons
  • Fatigue-reducing boots
  • Weather-resistant cloaks
  • Minor elemental arrows
  • Reliable-quality potions
  • Specialist field tools

Uncommon Grade items are carried by competent adventurers and professional fighters. For lower-ranked guild members, acquiring one may mark the first meaningful step toward serious adventuring.

3
Approximately 1 in 500

Rare Grade

Grade 3 items are rare enough to be recorded, appraised carefully and sometimes fought over by local powers. They often possess significant magical effects or craftsmanship beyond normal production.

Rare Grade items may influence duels, monster hunts, expeditions and small-scale battles.

  • Elementally empowered weapons
  • Magic-absorbing shields
  • Mana-control rings
  • Damage-resistant armor
  • Relics of fallen noble houses
  • Elite magical tools

They are commonly associated with experienced adventurers, guild officers, talented knights and wealthy collectors. The item remains mortal-made or mortal-usable, but has crossed into true magical distinction.

4
Approximately 1 in 5,000

Epic Grade

Grade 4 items are powerful artifacts capable of influencing major missions, regional conflicts or elite combat encounters. They are often tied to historic battles, master artisans, ancient magical schools or forgotten ruins.

Epic Grade items are difficult to create, difficult to replace and rarely sold openly.

  • Stone-cutting weapons
  • Self-repairing armor
  • Storm-summoning staffs
  • Curse-warding relics
  • Crowd-affecting instruments
  • Large-scale ritual tools

Guilds and kingdoms record known Epic items within their territories. Possession may let an adventurer fight above their ordinary rank, though an artifact cannot replace skill, discipline or courage.

5
Approximately 1 in 50,000

Grand Grade

Grade 5 artifacts are objects of exceptional power, historical value and magical depth, usually associated with legendary figures, royal treasuries, ancient temples or major world events.

A Grand Grade item can influence battles, diplomacy and the fate of noble houses.

  • Terrain-reshaping weapons
  • Command-enhancing crowns
  • Entity-summoning relics
  • Dragonfire-resistant armor
  • Instruments of ancient divine rites
  • Impossible alchemical vessels

Grand items are rarely owned by ordinary individuals for long.

Kingdoms seek them. Guilds track them. Thieves dream of them.

Lancers may ignore them, but mortals build legends around them.

6
Approximately 1 in 500,000

Legendary Grade

Grade 6 relics are powerful enough to alter wars, reshape political borders or establish the reputation of entire bloodlines.

Many are named. Some are worshipped. Others are sealed away because their use is considered too dangerous.

  • Dragon-forged weapons
  • Regional weather relics
  • Time-bending staffs
  • Divine-resistant armor
  • Great ancient ritual objects
  • Weapons of national heroes

Legendary items are often associated with Mithril or Orichalcum adventurers, elite royal champions and ancient orders.

To possess one is to become part of history.

To misuse one is to invite catastrophe.

7
Approximately 1 in 5,000,000

Mythic Grade

Grade 7 artifacts are so rare that many are known only through songs, ruins, religious disputes or contradictory chronicles.

They approach the edge of divine craftsmanship while remaining theoretically usable by mortals of sufficient strength, preparation or compatibility.

  • Dimension-cutting swords
  • Spell-negating armor
  • Titan-forged relics
  • Compasses for hidden planes
  • Limited fate-altering instruments
  • Artifacts that seal lesser rifts

Mythic items are most often associated with Orichalcum and Adamantite adventurers. A weaker mortal may survive attempting to wield one, but is unlikely to control it properly.

At this level, artifacts stop being possessions. They become relationships.

8
One of a kind

Fabled Grade

Each Grade 8 relic is unique.

There are no copies, equivalents or replacements. These singular artifacts are tied to divine events, impossible craftsmanship, primordial accidents, lost ages or entities beyond ordinary history.

Unlike Grade 7 items, Fabled relics stand alone. Their existence shapes myths, wars, cults, dynasties and prophecies.

  • Abodeer’s Coins
  • The Primordial Flame
  • The Torch of the TorchBearer
  • The Seven Crafts of Hecarim

A Fabled item may not always exceed every lower artifact in destructive output, but it possesses an irreplaceable nature.

Its uniqueness is part of its power.

To lose one is to lose a piece of history that cannot return.

9
Divine-level manifestation

Primordial Grade

GodsLancersBlighted LancersEmissaries

Grade 9 artifacts contain direct divine resonance, primordial authority or Spherebound manifestation. They exist at a level ordinary mortals were never meant to touch.

If an ordinary mortal attempts to wield one, the result is usually fatal. The mortal body cannot withstand the resonance, and the soul risks being violently severed from life and returned to the cycle.

  • The Lance manifested during RULE
  • The personal weapon of a Lancer
  • An Emissary’s instrument or sacred item
  • Divine implements of the Spheres
  • Blighted resonance weapons

Primordial items are not acquired through treasure hunting. They are granted, manifested, inherited through divine authority or produced through direct Spherebound resonance.

A mortal may look upon such an item.

They may even survive standing near one.

But to claim one without the proper metaphysical nature is to invite obliteration.

X
Beyond itemhood · Known form: Hymns

Unobtainable Grade

Grade X is the highest and most mysterious classification in Aulon’s artifact system.

It is also, in a strict sense, misleading.

Grade X objects are called items only because mortal scholars require a category for them. In truth, they are not items at all.

They are Hymns.

A Hymn is not a weapon, relic, tool or artifact. It is a sound of creation—the voice of a Sphere, the resonance of cosmic law or the echo of the primordial language through which reality was shaped.

Hymns cannot be owned.

They cannot be forged.

They cannot be sold.

They cannot be locked in vaults.

They can only be heard, invoked, remembered, misinterpreted or briefly manifested through impossible circumstances.

The True Sound heard during RULE approaches this category, for when a Lancer invokes their Sphere’s full resonance, the Hymn of that Sphere echoes across the world.

Yet even then, the Lancer does not possess the Hymn.

The Hymn passes through them.

Grade X represents the unreachable: things so fundamental that to hold them would be to hold the laws of existence itself.

THE HIGHEST CLASSIFICATIONS

Grade 8, Grade 9 and Grade X

Grade 8 · Obtainable

Unique relic

One of a kind and historically irreplaceable, yet still capable of being found, held, stolen, hidden or used.

Grade 9 · Bestowed

Divine manifestation

Granted or manifested for beings capable of withstanding primordial resonance. Mortals cannot safely wield it.

Grade X · Witnessed

Cosmic phenomenon

A Hymn or law that cannot be possessed at all.

Grade 8 may be obtained.
Grade 9 may be granted or manifested.
Grade X may only be witnessed.

RULE AND PRIMORDIAL ITEMS

Temporary divine itemhood

When a High Lancer activates RULE, their resonance manifests a Lance classified as a Grade 9 Primordial Item.

This Lance is temporary. It exists only for the duration of the RULE and vanishes once the imposed law has been completed or exhausted.

This is why RULE is not simply a technique.

It is the temporary manifestation of divine itemhood through perfect resonance.

The Lancer becomes worthy to receive a Primordial Item because, for that brief moment, their soul is in complete alignment with their patron Sphere.

The Lance does not belong to them permanently.

It arrives because reality recognizes their authority.

Then it disappears when that authority has been spent.

THE SCHOLAR’S WARNING

Power is not possession

Many adventurers believe power lies in possessing higher-grade artifacts.

This belief has killed more treasure hunters than monsters ever could.

An item’s grade measures not only its value, but also its danger.

  • A Grade 1 sword may serve anyone.
  • A Grade 5 relic may demand discipline.
  • A Grade 7 artifact may require compatibility.
  • A Grade 8 relic may change the course of one’s life.
  • A Grade 9 item may kill the unworthy instantly.
  • A Grade X Hymn may change the world simply by being heard.

Therefore, the first rule taught by responsible relic scholars is simple:

Do not seek what your soul cannot bear.
